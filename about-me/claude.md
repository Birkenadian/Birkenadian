# About Andrew Cashin

Standing context for all projects. This file holds public-facing information only, because this repository is public. Personal details beyond what appears in Andrew's published bios are deliberately excluded.

## Who Andrew is

Andrew Alexander Cashin. Born in Merseyside, UK, emigrated to Canada in 1981, Canadian citizen, lives in Toronto. Twenty-five years in corporate event development and experience design. Certified meditation and mindfulness teacher since 2015. Lifelong Liverpool F.C. supporter and co-founder and founding Chairman of the Official Liverpool Supporters Club of Toronto. Co-founder of the East Side Vinyl Society, referenced only when truly necessary. Record collector for more than forty years, from Merseyside record shops in the late seventies to weekly crate digging in Toronto. Musical territory: new wave, punk, hardcore, jazz, alternative, electronic, classical, indie, funk, and world.

Andrew is rolling his events, writing, record collecting, and mindfulness work under one brand: ZEN records.

## The first book

*Zen and the Art of Record Collecting* by Andrew Cashin, published by Quarto Publishing in early 2027, with a foreword by broadcaster Alan Cross. The book treats record collecting as a mindful practice and explores the phenomenology of collecting: the felt experience of listening, searching, and remembering.

Endorsements have come from Kevin Lewandowski (Founder and CEO of Discogs), Hanuman Welch (Apple Music 1), Michael Kurtz (co-founder of Record Store Day), Martin Popoff, Andrew Cartmel, Eric Alper, Jeff Barber (Sonic Boom Music, Toronto), David Sax, Paul Myers, Jonathan Scott, Daniel Richler, Richard Blade, Guy Kawasaki, Laurie Brown, Andrew Rossiter (Org Music), and Jack Coyne (Track Star). Full blurb texts are on file with Andrew; quote them exactly or not at all.

## Record collecting philosophy

There are no must-have records. A collection built from someone else's must-have list is a collection built from someone else's taste, and it will not feel like yours, for the simple reason that it is not. Record collections are fingerprints; no two are the same. The shelf is the geography of your listening life. Never buy a record you will not listen to. The size of the collection matters less than the quality of attention you bring to what is on the shelf. Never think of your collection as an investment. Think of each record as a companion, a teacher, a friend who tells you something different every time you drop the needle. Look after them, and they will look after you.
