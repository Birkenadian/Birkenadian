# Needle Drop: A Year of Listening at Random (Expanded Concept)

An expanded treatment of concept 1 from the book two pitch document.

## The premise

Beginning January 1, 2027, Andrew listens to one album every day for a year, and he never chooses it. Each morning the Discogs random item feature deals him a record from his own collection, and whatever comes up gets played, front to back, with full attention. The book is the diary of that year: 365 dated entries, one per day, each a small act of listening recorded while it is still warm.

The randomness carries the meaning. Recommendation engines feed us more of whatever we already like. Needle Drop uses an algorithm to bring chance, surprise, and surrender back into daily listening. Some days the dice deal a favourite. Some days they deal a record he forgot he owned, or one he has been avoiding for twenty years. The practice is showing up anyway. That is where the mindfulness lives: non-attachment, beginner's mind, and the discipline of sitting with what you are given.

## Each day's entry

Every entry occupies its place in the calendar, so the book reads from January 1 to December 31. Each day opens with the front cover art, reproduced for every entry, which gives the book its visual rhythm and makes it a heavily illustrated object. Beside the art sits the version listened to, identified the way Discogs identifies it: label, year, country, format, and the pressing details collectors love, from matrix etchings to the story of how this copy entered the collection. A tight paragraph covers the record itself, the sessions, the context, and the reason it matters. The heart of each entry is an honest account of how the listen felt, written in the warm, reflective voice of the first book. Memory, resistance, boredom, joy: whatever actually surfaced goes on the page.

Entries run short by design, roughly 250 to 300 words plus the artwork, so the book rewards cover-to-cover reading and the bedside dip alike. You can read today's date in any year you own it.

## Structure

Twelve chapters, one per month, each opening with a one-page reflection on a theme the month's listening surfaced, such as chance, repetition, mood, listening while sad, or the records we keep and never play. A short introduction lays out the rules of the game, and a closing essay looks back at what a year of surrendered choice did to a collector's relationship with his own shelves.

The rules give the book its charm and its honesty. There are no re-rolls, with one publicly confessed exception allowed per month. Box sets get one side per day until they are done. A record that comes up twice gets listened to twice, and written about differently the second time.

## The foreword

Kevin Lewandowski, Founder and CEO of Discogs, is the natural foreword writer, since his platform's random item feature deals the cards each morning. He has already endorsed Zen and the Art of Record Collecting, so the relationship exists. A Discogs connection also opens promotional possibilities worth exploring with their team, because the book doubles as a year-long love letter to cataloguing your collection.

## Why this version beats the weekly version

The weekly practices version asked the reader to do the work. This version lets the reader watch the work being done, with a narrative, a character, and a year that has an arc, while still modelling a practice anyone can copy. A one-page appendix, "Run Your Own Random Year," makes the invitation explicit. The calendar gives it evergreen gift appeal. The cover art makes it a beautiful object, squarely in an illustrated publisher's wheelhouse. And the daily entry format means the manuscript gets written day by day through 2027, the same year the first book arrives, so the two books could feed each other's audiences.
