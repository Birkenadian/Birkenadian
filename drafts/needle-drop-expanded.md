# Needle Drop: A Year of Listening at Random — Expanded Concept

*An expanded treatment of concept #1 from the book two pitch document.*

## The premise

Beginning January 1st, 2027, Andrew listens to one album every day for a year — and he doesn't choose it. Each morning, the Discogs "random item" feature deals him a record from his own collection, and whatever comes up gets played, front to back, with full attention. The book is the diary of that year: 365 dated entries, one per day, each a small act of listening recorded while it's still warm.

The randomness is the heart of it. We live inside recommendation engines that serve us more of what we already like; *Needle Drop* uses an algorithm to do the opposite — to restore chance, surprise, and surrender to daily listening. Some days the dice deal a desert-island favourite; some days a record he forgot he owned, or one he's been avoiding for twenty years. The practice is showing up anyway. That's where the mindfulness lives: non-attachment, beginner's mind, and the discipline of sitting with what you're given rather than what you'd have picked.

## Each day's entry

Every entry occupies its place in the calendar — the book reads January 1 to December 31 — and includes:

- **The front cover art**, reproduced for every entry. This is a heavily illustrated book; the covers carry the visual rhythm of the year.
- **The version listened to** — the specific pressing as identified on Discogs: label, year, country, format, and the pressing details collectors love (matrix etchings, repress vs. original, the story of how this copy entered the collection).
- **Significant information about the record** — a tight paragraph of history: the sessions, the context, the reason it matters (or doesn't, which can be just as interesting).
- **How it made him feel** — the contemplative core. Not a review and not a rating: what surfaced during the listen — memory, resistance, boredom, joy — written in the warm, reflective voice of book one.

Entries run short by design (roughly 250–300 words plus the artwork), so the book invites both cover-to-cover reading and the bedside dip — read today's date, any year you own it.

## Structure

Twelve chapters, one per month, each opening with a one-page reflection on a theme the month's listening surfaced — chance, repetition, mood versus music, listening while sad, the records we keep but never play. A short introduction lays out the rules of the game, and a closing essay looks back at what a year of surrendered choice did to a collector's relationship with his own shelves.

The "rules of the game," stated up front, give the book its charm and its honesty: no re-rolls (with one publicly confessed exception allowed per month), box sets get one side per day until done, doubles that come up twice get listened to twice — and written about differently the second time.

## The foreword

Kevin Lewandowski, founder and CEO of Discogs — whose database and random-item feature literally deal the cards each morning — is the natural foreword writer, and he has already supported *Zen and the Art of Record Collecting* with a blurb, so the relationship exists. A Discogs connection also opens promotional possibilities worth exploring with their team: the book is, among other things, a year-long love letter to cataloguing your collection.

## Why this version is stronger than the original pitch

The weekly-practices version asked the reader to do the work; this version lets the reader watch the work being done — a narrative, a character, and a year with an arc, while still modelling a practice anyone can copy ("run your own random year" sits naturally as a one-page appendix). The calendar gives it evergreen gift appeal; the cover art makes it a beautiful object, squarely in an illustrated publisher's wheelhouse; and the entry format makes the manuscript genuinely writable day by day during 2027.

## Open questions for Dennis

1. **Art licensing.** 365 cover reproductions is the book's biggest cost and clearance question — what's Quarto's appetite and process for that volume of imagery?
2. **Timeline.** If the listening year is 2027, the manuscript completes in early 2028. Is that runway comfortable, or should entries begin sooner so the calendar year is editorial rather than literal?
3. **Scope.** All 365 entries at full size, or a "best of the year" selection (say, 120 full entries with a complete day-by-day index)? Page count drives format and price.
4. **Trim and treatment.** Page-a-day chunky hardcover, illustrated gift format, or something between — and is there interest in a companion page-a-day calendar product down the line?
