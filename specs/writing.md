# Writing Spec: Book Two Pitch Document for Dennis at Quarto

## Objective

A pitch document, sent as an email attachment with a short cover email, that gives Dennis at Quarto 4–6 developed concepts for Andrew Cashin's second book. Context: Dennis is currently managing publication of Andrew's first book, *Zen and the Art of Record Collecting* (Quarto) — a blend of practical advice and philosophical reflection that treats record collecting as a mindful practice. Andrew and Dennis recently agreed to swap ideas for a follow-up; neither has proposed anything yet, and Andrew wants to get something to Dennis soon, while the exchange is fresh.

The document must stay squarely in Andrew's lane — record collecting, music, hi-fi and listening culture, mindfulness, and meditation — and read as a natural continuation of the voice and sensibility of book one. It is a conversation-starter for an editor who already knows and works with the author, not a cold formal proposal.

## Requirements

1. Produce two files: the pitch document at `drafts/book2-pitches.md` and the cover email draft at `drafts/cover-email-dennis.md`.
2. The pitch document contains between 4 and 6 book concepts, inclusive.
3. Every concept stays within the lane: record collecting, music, listening/hi-fi culture, mindfulness, or meditation (or a combination). No concepts outside these subjects.
4. Each concept includes exactly these four elements, clearly labeled or structurally obvious:
   a. A working title.
   b. A hook of 2–4 sentences describing the concept and why a reader would want it.
   c. The target reader, in 1–2 sentences.
   d. A bridge: 1–2 sentences on how it builds on *Zen and the Art of Record Collecting*.
5. Each concept additionally includes one sentence stating how it is distinct from book one — why it is its own book rather than a rehash.
6. Concepts are presented in ranked order, strongest first, and the document opens with a 1–2 sentence note explaining why the #1 concept leads.
7. Each concept runs 150–250 words; the full pitch document runs 900–1,600 words including any intro/outro.
8. The pitch document opens with a brief (≤75 words) framing paragraph reminding Dennis of the context (follow-up ideas, as discussed) — it must stand alone if Dennis forwards it internally at Quarto.
9. Tone throughout matches book one's sensibility: warm, practical-meets-contemplative, conversational-professional. No hard-sell marketing language.
10. The cover email is ≤150 words, addresses Dennis by first name, references their recent "any ideas?" exchange, says the ideas are attached, invites his reaction, and does not propose or commit to any dates or deadlines.
11. Both files are plain Markdown, with the pitch document formatted cleanly enough to convert directly to an attachment (clear headings, no raw URLs, no code blocks).

## Constraints

- **No invented facts.** No sales figures, print runs, endorsements, quotes, statistics, or claims about book one's performance or reception. The only facts about book one that may be referenced: its title, publisher (Quarto), subject matter, practical-plus-philosophical approach, and forewords by Alan Cross and Lodro Rinzler.
- **No fabricated credentials or biography** for Andrew beyond: author of *Zen and the Art of Record Collecting*, record collector, based in Toronto.
- Do not presume a series or sequel commitment from Quarto; concepts may *suggest* series potential but must not assume it.
- Do not disparage book one, other authors, other books, streaming, or digital music; the lane's spirit is appreciation, not gatekeeping.
- Mindfulness/meditation content must stay secular and accessible — no religious instruction, no claims of therapeutic or medical benefit.
- No placeholder text, no "TBD," no bracketed gaps in either file.
- Timing: this is wanted soon; the build should complete the deliverable in one pass without requesting additional research from the user.

## Edge Cases

- **Overlap with book one.** The most natural ideas risk being book one again with a new cover. Handle via Requirement 5: every concept must state its distinction in one sentence; any concept that cannot is excluded.
- **Unknown commercial performance.** Book one's sales are unknown (it is currently in publication). No concept may justify itself by book one's success ("building on the success of...") — bridges must be about subject and voice, not sales.
- **Dennis's surname and title are unknown.** The email addresses him as "Dennis" only; no invented surname, no job title.
- **"Zen" framing.** Book one uses Zen in the accessible, Pirsig-echoing sense. Concepts may reuse a "Zen and the Art of..." style title for at most one concept; the rest must demonstrate range beyond the formula.
- **Mixed audience for the attachment.** Dennis may forward the document to colleagues who don't know Andrew. The framing paragraph (Requirement 8) must carry enough context that the document makes sense without the email.
- **Reader knowledge floor.** Concepts must be pitched so a non-collector editor understands the appeal; any collecting or hi-fi jargon used in a hook must be self-explanatory in context.
